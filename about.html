About Lexicon Earth

A local-first atlas of word histories — search a word, see a schematic journey across languages and places on a globe.

What this is

Lexicon Earth helps you explore how English words connect to earlier forms in other languages. It plots those forms on a map using language heartlands (approximate geographic associations), not attested travel itineraries.

Lookups use free public dictionary APIs. Results you open are stored in your browser (IndexedDB) so starter words and recent searches can work offline.

What it is not

  • Not a professional etymological dictionary or a substitute for scholarly sources.
  • Not a literal map of how a word “traveled” from place to place.
  • Not a guarantee that every parsed template or reconstructed form is correct.

Read the globe as a sketch. Routes are schematic. Markers sit on language heartlands so related forms are easy to compare — they are not GPS pins for historical speech events.

Data sources

  • English Wiktionary — primary etymology extraction (CC BY-SA 4.0)
  • Glottolog — language heartland coordinates for the atlas (CC BY 4.0; Hammarström, Forkel, Haspelmath & Bank)
  • Wikidata Lexemes — optional structured links (CC0)
  • Kaikki / Wiktextract — optional assist (CC BY-SA 4.0)
  • Natural Earth — land & coastline (public domain)
  • three.js — 3D rendering (MIT)

Map pins use language heartlands or centroids — not attested travel routes. Approximate, reconstructed, and period-only positions are labeled on each stage. See language atlas attribution for rebuild details and license notes.

Each lookup lists its citations in the Sources panel, including a link back to the Wiktionary entry when available.

Local-first design

No account. No API keys. No paid backend. Your cache stays on this device unless you export it. Clearing site data in the browser removes cached lineages.
